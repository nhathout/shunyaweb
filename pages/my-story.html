Alexandra “Sasha” Petrovicheva is finishing two degrees at Northeastern University — International Business and International Affairs — and graduates in May 2027. Seven years in home-health operations showed her how privacy and compliance decisions land on real people; co-ops at TJX and Wellington Management took her from brand strategy to information security and AI governance. Her focus is the place where artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, privacy, business and law meet. The path there: an MBA with an AI and technology focus, a few years in consulting, then law school.

The story, in three parts

01 Los Angeles roots

Sasha’s story starts in the Los Angeles area. In 2019 she joined Guardian Angel Home Health Agency in Simi Valley on the finance side — payroll, insurance documentation, recurring reporting — and seven years later she is still there, now as Business Operations Associate. It is where she first saw how privacy, compliance and insurance decisions land on real employees and patients.

The same years held three years of volunteering with Heal the Bay and the SCV Food Bank, and a 2022 legal internship at MCIS Lawyers in Thousand Oaks — legal research, case documents and close attention to procedural detail.

Continue in Community & Service · Law & Advocacy

02 Boston chapter

At Northeastern, Sasha studies across two colleges: International Business at the D’Amore-McKim School of Business and International Affairs at the College of Social Sciences and Humanities, with concentrations in Global Business Strategy and Business Analytics. She made the Dean’s List in Fall 2023 and Fall 2024.

Co-ops carried her from TJ Maxx social strategy at TJX to rotations across legal, risk, information security and AI governance at Wellington Management. On campus she led media relations for TEDxNortheasternU and served as Assistant Director of Communications for the Student Government Association.

Continue in AI & Governance · Leadership & Impact

03 What she is building

Her focus is the intersection of artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, data privacy, business and law. She is not aiming to be a software engineer; she wants to understand the technology well enough to help companies use it responsibly, manage risk and meet regulation.

That thread runs through her faculty-supervised research on governing agentic AI in financial operations, and through her work with NextWork, helping students from nontechnical disciplines build with AI. Next comes an MBA with an AI and technology focus in Fall 2027 — then consulting, then law school.
